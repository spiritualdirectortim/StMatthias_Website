# Voice and wit

How to sound like a person, and how to be funny without being cute. Names and details in
the examples are invented for teaching; real copy uses only the project's facts.

## 1. Why "personal" is a technique, not a mood

Readers decide in a sentence whether a human wrote this. Kahneman's substitution heuristic
explains the stakes: asked "should I act on this?", a reader quietly answers the easier
question "do I like and trust whoever is talking?" Institutional voice makes that answer
"nobody is talking", and nothing you say afterwards is weighed.

The moves that make writing personal are mechanical and learnable:

- **Second person, singular.** "You" is one reader. "Parishioners", "members", "families"
  and "everyone" are a crowd, and crowds do not act (Heath; Kahneman on identifiable
  victims).
- **A visible speaker.** "I" when one person writes, "we" when a real group does. Sign
  with a name. "The parish office" is a building.
- **The reader's nouns.** Say "the class", "the hall behind the church", "the 9:45", not
  "the program", "the facility", "the liturgy". Spool's trigger-word studies: pages that
  used the visitor's own words succeeded; pages that used the organization's failed.
- **Sugarman's test.** Read it aloud across a kitchen table. Any sentence you would not
  say to a friend gets rewritten until you would.
- **Specific over general, always.** "Donuts in the hall after the 9:45" is personal
  because it is a scene; "fellowship after Mass" is a category.
- **Admit something.** The honest limit ("parking is tight", "it is a Tuesday") is the
  fastest proof that a person is speaking and that the next claim can be trusted
  (Cialdini's weakness-first; Sugarman's honesty trigger).

## 2. The five sources of wit

Wit is precision plus a small surprise. It is never a joke bolted on. The five reliable
sources, in order of safety:

1. **The exact, unexpected detail.** "The coffee is better than it has any right to be."
   The humor is that someone noticed and said so. This is the safest kind because it is
   also the most concrete (Heath), so even readers who do not smile get information.
2. **Understatement where hype was expected.** "It is a Tuesday night. We know." The
   reader expected a sell; they got a shrug. Trust rises because the writer is not
   pushing (Voss's calm; Cialdini's authority-with-a-flaw).
3. **The honest admission.** "We lost the sign-up sheet. Here is a new one." People laugh
   at candor because it is rare, and candor is the honesty trigger doing double duty.
4. **The turn.** The last clause changes the meaning of the first. "Everyone is welcome,
   including people who are only here for the donuts." Put the turn at the end of the
   sentence; a turn in the middle gets lost.
5. **Saying the quiet part plainly.** "Yes, the roof. Again." The reader was already
   thinking it; saying it first is Voss's accusation audit with a smile.

Wit that fails and why:
- **Puns in headlines** cost a second of parsing and usually cost clarity; the headline's
  only job is the first sentence (Sugarman, Schwartz).
- **Exclamation marks and "fun" adjectives** ("a FUN-tastic evening!") raise the pitch
  of the voice and signal effort; effort reads as doubt (Kahneman's fluency).
- **Sarcasm** needs tone of voice to land; on a page, 7-38-55 collapses to the 7 (Voss).
- **Jokes about the reader** or about any group break liking and unity (Cialdini) in one
  line.
- **Three jokes in a row** make the piece a performance; one wink makes it a person.

## 3. Where wit goes and where it never goes

Place the one moment of wit in the deck, mid-body, or the last line. Keep it out of the
headline unless it is also the clearest possible headline. Keep it off the button.

Never joke about: money trouble, grief, illness or death, someone's faith or doubt,
bodies, age, families' circumstances, or the reader's absence ("long time no see"). In a
piece about any of those, be warm and plain and let the detail carry the humanity.

When unsure, choose warmth. A warm line that is not funny still works. A joke that misses
makes the reader distrust the warmth too.

## 4. The tone dial: Voss's three voices on the page

Voss names three negotiating voices. Each has a written equivalent.

| Voice | When | On the page |
|---|---|---|
| Late-night FM DJ | Hard asks, bad news, upset readers, money | Short declaratives. Periods, not exclamation marks. Downward inflection: sentences that land, not lift. Slow pacing: one idea per sentence, white space. |
| Positive, playful | Most web copy, invitations, newsletters | Light sentences, one wink, warmth in the specifics. Smiling while you write changes word choice; do it. |
| Direct, assertive | Almost never in copy | Commands without warmth read as pressure. Use only for safety or logistics ("Park on the street, not in the fire lane."). |

Default to playful for invitations and DJ for anything that touches loss, apology or money.
Never mix them in one paragraph; the reader hears a mood swing.

## 5. Rewrites, graded

Each row shows a corporate line, a "trying too hard" line that overshoots, and the line
that works, with the move that makes it work.

| Corporate | Overshoot | Works | Move |
|---|---|---|---|
| We invite all parishioners to our annual parish picnic. | Get ready for the MOST EPIC picnic EVER!!! | The picnic is Sunday after the 9:45. Bring a dish and a chair; we have the shade. | Specific scene; "we have the shade" is the detail that smiles. |
| Thank you for your continued generosity. | You guys are AMAZING and we literally could not do this without you! | Sixty households gave last month. The heat stayed on, the lights stayed on, and nobody had to ask twice. Thank you. | Real number, concrete consequence, understatement. |
| New parishioners are encouraged to register at their earliest convenience. | Don't be a stranger, newbie! Sign up today! | If you have been coming a while and never registered, you are not alone, and nobody is keeping score. It takes a minute and lets us know your name. | Label the reader's hesitation; admit; small step. |
| Volunteers are needed for the hospitality ministry. | Calling all coffee heroes! | Welcome Weekend needs two more people who can pour coffee and say good morning. That is the whole job description. | Honest, specific, the wit is in the shortness of the list. |
| Due to unforeseen circumstances, the 10 a.m. event has been cancelled. We apologize for any inconvenience. | Oops! Looks like we goofed. | We cancelled the 10 a.m. and told almost no one. If you drove over, that was our mistake, and we would like to make it up to you: what would help? | Accusation audit, plain ownership, calibrated question. |

## 6. A self-test

Before shipping, read the piece and answer:

1. Could the reader tell this was written by a person, and roughly what that person is
   like, from the first two sentences?
2. Is there one line a friend might read aloud to someone else? Is it kind?
3. Would a tired person reading on a phone smile, or squint?
4. Delete the wittiest line. Does the piece still work? (It should. If the wit is
   load-bearing, the argument is not.)
5. Is the tone one voice from first line to last?
