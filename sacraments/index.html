Sacraments

Encounter Christ in the sacraments.

Sacraments are outward signs of God’s love, instituted by Christ and entrusted to the Church, through which God’s grace is given to his people by the work of the Holy Spirit. Here is how to prepare for each of the seven at St. Matthias.

Baptism Reconciliation First Communion & Eucharist Confirmation Marriage Anointing of the Sick Holy Orders & Vocations

The seven sacraments

Grace for every stage of life.

The Catholic Church celebrates seven sacraments, grouped by their purpose:

  • Sacraments of initiation: Baptism, Confirmation, and the Eucharist, which together make a person a full member of the Church.
  • Sacraments of healing: Reconciliation and the Anointing of the Sick.
  • Sacraments of service: Holy Orders and Marriage.

The sacraments are free, just as God’s love is free. To begin preparing for any of them, call the parish office at (650) 366-9544 or email info@stmatthiasparish.org.

Sacrament of initiation

Baptism

The foundation of the Christian life and the first of the seven sacraments.

What it is

Baptism is the first step into the Christian life. The word comes from the Greek baptizein, meaning to “plunge” or “immerse” in water. Through Baptism we are freed from original sin, reborn as children of God, made members of Christ, and welcomed into the family of the Church. With Confirmation and the Eucharist, it is one of the three sacraments of Christian initiation.

Who it is for

  • Infants and young children, whose parents ask for Baptism for them.
  • Children age 7 and older who have not been baptized. They prepare with their families through a process adapted for children.
  • Adults, who prepare through Becoming Catholic.

How preparation works

Infants and young children

Parents begin with an informational appointment with Deacon Rich Foley. At that meeting, parents who have not attended a baptism preparation class within the last few years sign up for one, and the date of the Baptism is set. Baptisms are usually celebrated at a weekend Mass, Saturday at 5:00 p.m. or Sunday at 9:45 a.m., so the whole community can welcome the child and support the family. A baptismal certificate is mailed to the parents afterward.

Godparents

Godparents help parents raise their child in the Catholic faith and represent the faith community. They must be at least 16 years old, and at least one godparent must be a fully initiated Catholic (baptized, confirmed, and receiving the Eucharist). Godparents are welcome at the preparation class but are not required to attend.

Older children

Families of school-age children who have not been baptized enroll in the parish’s family faith formation program and begin attending together. Children prepare for all three sacraments of initiation—Baptism, Confirmation, and First Communion—and parents take an active part.

Next step: Call the parish office at (650) 366-9544 to set up an informational appointment.

Contact: Deacon Rich Foley (infants and young children) or Carmen Perlas (children 7 and older), through the parish office.

Sacrament of healing

Reconciliation

Also called confession or Penance.

What it is

The Sacrament of Reconciliation is a way to experience God’s mercy and forgiveness, offered to us through the Church. In it, we confess our sins to a priest, receive absolution, and are reconciled with God and the Church.

Who it is for

Any baptized Catholic who wants to receive God’s forgiveness. Children receive the sacrament for the first time as they prepare for First Communion.

How preparation works

Reconciliation is available by appointment during the week. Call the parish office to schedule a time and place.

Children prepare as part of the parish’s faith formation program, usually in second grade. Preparation includes a family retreat in November, and children celebrate their first Reconciliation with their families each January.

Next step: Call (650) 366-9544 to schedule an appointment for confession.

Contact: the parish office; for children’s preparation, Carmen Perlas.

Sacrament of initiation

First Communion & Eucharist

“The source and summit of the Christian life.”

What it is

In the Eucharist, Jesus Christ gives himself to us as the Bread of Life. The Catechism of the Catholic Church calls the Eucharist “the source and summit of the Christian life”: every other sacrament and ministry of the Church is bound up with it and directed toward it. We celebrate the Eucharist at every Mass, and First Communion is the first time a person receives it.

Who it is for

  • Children around age 7, usually in second grade, who have completed at least one full year of faith formation at St. Matthias or another parish.
  • Older children (fourth grade and up), who also complete one full year of faith formation before sacramental preparation begins.
  • Adults who have not received First Communion, who prepare through Becoming Catholic.

How preparation works

Families who want these sacraments for their children enroll in and regularly attend GIFT (Generations in Faith Together), the parish’s family faith formation program. Children prepare for Reconciliation and First Communion together, with two family retreats: one in November for Reconciliation and one in February or March for the Eucharist. First Reconciliation is celebrated in January, and First Communion at the end of April or the beginning of May.

Each October, letters with important dates are sent to families with children interested in these sacraments. Every family’s situation is different, so please reach out with any questions about your child’s readiness.

Next step: Enroll your family in GIFT and contact the parish office about First Communion preparation.

Contact: Carmen Perlas, through the parish office.

Sacrament of initiation

Confirmation

Strengthened by the Holy Spirit to be witnesses of Christ.

What it is

Confirmation completes the grace of Baptism. Through the laying on of hands and anointing with sacred oil (chrism), the person being confirmed receives the gift of the Holy Spirit and is strengthened to spread and defend the faith in word and action. Like Baptism, Confirmation is received only once.

Who it is for

  • High school students who have been baptized, have received First Communion, and are actively living their faith.
  • Adults who have been baptized but not confirmed.

How preparation works

Teens: a two-year program

Year 1, Discernment, is a time to connect with the parish, become more involved in ministry and service, choose a sponsor and meet with them monthly, and complete service hours. Once candidates have completed Year 1 and discerned that they are ready, they move on to Year 2, Discipleship, which focuses on growing in a personal relationship with Jesus through individual and communal prayer, with guidance from parents, sponsors, and the parish community.

Service

Candidates complete 20 hours of service: 10 in the parish, such as helping at church, at GIFT gatherings, or at parish events, and 10 in the wider community, such as volunteering at a food bank, tutoring, or running errands for a sick neighbor.

Sponsors

  • Over 16 years old and fully initiated (baptized, confirmed, and receiving the Eucharist)
  • Someone other than the candidate’s parent
  • Active in their own parish community
  • Living nearby, so they can attend events with the candidate

Adults

Adults have two options: prepare through Becoming Catholic at St. Matthias, or join the Archdiocese of San Francisco’s adult Confirmation preparation, a Friday evening and Saturday session offered in the fall and repeated in the spring. Archdiocesan candidates are confirmed at Pentecost at the Cathedral of St. Mary of the Assumption in San Francisco.

Next step: Contact the parish office to register a teen for Confirmation preparation or to talk through options as an adult.

Contact: Carmen Perlas, through the parish office.

Sacrament of service

Marriage

Celebrating your love and the God who is its author.

What it is

Marriage in the Catholic Church is more than the celebration of a couple’s love for each other. The Church rejoices in your love and also recognizes God as its author. Preparation helps make your wedding not only a time of great happiness but also an expression of faith in the God in whose love you were created.

Who it is for

Couples who wish to marry in the Catholic Church. If you would like to be married at St. Matthias, it is desirable that at least one of you live in the parish and be a parishioner. If neither of you does, you will need permission from your own pastor. A Catholic may marry someone who is not Catholic at St. Matthias; any questions about your situation are worked through with the parish clergy.

How preparation works

  • Contact the parish at least six months before your proposed date. This is a policy of the Archdiocese of San Francisco and cannot be waived by the parish.
  • Meet with a priest or deacon. At your first meeting you will learn each step of preparation. Both partners should be willing and able to take part in the whole process.
  • Gather documents. A Catholic needs a baptismal certificate issued within the last six months, available from the church where you were baptized. A baptized partner who is not Catholic brings a record of their baptism.

Next step: Call (650) 366-9544 to schedule a first meeting.

Contact: Deacon Rich Foley, through the parish office.

Sacrament of healing

Anointing of the Sick

Christ’s healing and strength in times of illness.

What it is

Following the Letter of James, which tells the sick to call on the elders of the Church to pray over them and anoint them with oil in the name of the Lord, the Church offers this sacrament for strength, peace, and healing.

Who it is for

Anyone preparing for surgery, facing a serious illness, or in need of healing for a health-related issue. Please don’t wait until an illness is so advanced that the person cannot take part fully.

How it works

  • Individual anointing: call the parish office to arrange a time. When possible, the sacrament is celebrated during or after Mass so the community can take part.
  • Communal celebration: the parish celebrates the Anointing of the Sick together each year during Holy Week.
  • Home and hospital visits: parishioners regularly bring Holy Communion to people who are sick at home, in hospitals, or in care facilities, and a priest or deacon can visit on request.
  • In the hospital: for a priest at Kaiser or Sequoia hospitals, contact the hospital chaplain through the nurses’ station.

Next step: Call (650) 366-9544 to request the sacrament, Communion, or a visit.

Contact: the parish office.

Sacrament of service

Holy Orders & Vocations

“Speak, for your servant is listening.” (1 Samuel 3:10)

What it is

Through Holy Orders, men are ordained as deacons, priests, and bishops to serve the Church. Every ordination begins with a vocation. The word comes from the Latin vocare, “to call.” We usually choose careers based on our gifts and interests, but a vocation in the Church is something God calls us to live, whether as a priest, a religious sister or brother, or a parent, using our gifts in service to the world in the name of Christ.

Who it is for

Anyone who senses that God may be calling them to the priesthood or religious life. Some signs that the Lord may be at work in your life:

  • A personal relationship with Jesus Christ is important to you.
  • People have told you that you would make a good priest, sister, or brother.
  • Mass and the sacraments matter deeply to you.
  • The thought of priesthood or religious life keeps coming back.
  • You feel called to give more of yourself to others and to help people grow closer to Christ.

How discernment works

Discernment means using your heart and your head, and the wisdom of others, to recognize God’s call. Begin with prayer, then take a few practical steps:

  • Pray daily about God’s call in your life, and consider a vocation discernment retreat.
  • Talk with a parish priest or with the religious community you feel drawn to.
  • Learn about the differences between religious orders, communities, dioceses, and apostolates.
  • Contact the Office of Vocations of the Archdiocese of San Francisco.

Next step: Call the parish office to talk with one of our priests, or visit the Archdiocese of San Francisco (opens in a new tab).

Contact: Fr. Roy Remo or Fr. Al Nambatac, through the parish office.
