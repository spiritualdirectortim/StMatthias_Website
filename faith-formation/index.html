Faith Formation

Grow in faith at every age.

St. Matthias offers adult, intergenerational, and family-based faith formation for seekers at every stage of faith and from every walk of life.

Children & Families Youth Adults Becoming Catholic

Our approach

Faith grows best at home and together.

Parents matter most in shaping the faith of their children, so our programs give special attention to the family as the “domestic church,” the first place where faith is lived and shared. Families with children preparing for the sacraments go through preparation together.

Following the Church’s National Directory for Catechesis, every program aims to help people know the faith, understand the liturgy and sacraments, grow in a moral life in Christ, learn to pray, live as part of a community, and carry the Gospel into the world.

Because St. Matthias is a small parish, we can shape our programs around the people who take part in them.

Children & Families

Learning the faith side by side.

Contact Carmen Perlas, Religious Education Coordinator, through the parish office at (650) 366-9544.

GIFT: Generations in Faith Together

GIFT is the parish’s intergenerational, family-based faith formation program. All families with children in kindergarten through eighth grade are invited to register and attend together. GIFT gatherings are held on Sunday mornings from September to May, about twice a month, from 11:00 a.m. to 12:15 p.m. in Fr. Lacey Hall, and snacks are provided.

The curriculum is rooted in the Bible. It follows the Faith Stories series in a three-year cycle: God Calls introduces the Creator and the Hebrew Scriptures, Jesus Lives presents the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus in the Gospels, and Spirit With Us traces the work of the Holy Spirit through the Acts of the Apostles, the letters of the New Testament, and the lives of saints and modern believers.

GIFT is also the foundation for sacrament preparation. Children preparing for First Communion take part in GIFT in addition to their sacramental preparation.

Promise: Children’s Liturgy of the Word

Promise is a Children’s Liturgy of the Word for children ages 3 to 6, offered during the 9:45 a.m. Sunday Mass. Children celebrate the Liturgy of the Word, including a homily at their level, in a room near the church, then rejoin their families. Promise is run entirely by volunteers, so how often it is offered depends on how many parents and parishioners help. Volunteers are trained in the parish.

Preparing for the sacraments

Children prepare for the sacraments with their families as part of faith formation. See the steps for each on the Sacraments page:

  • First Reconciliation and First Communion, usually in second grade
  • Baptism for children 7 and older
  • Confirmation, a two-year program for high school students

Keeping children safe

In keeping with the Charter for the Protection of Children and Young People, every catechist and volunteer who works regularly with young people is fingerprinted or background-checked and completes the Protecting God’s Children course before beginning, renewed every three years. Each year, children also take part in age-appropriate lessons from the Empowering God’s Children program.

Next step: Call the parish office to register your family for GIFT, or to volunteer with Promise.

Youth

A place for teens to belong, serve, and ask big questions.

Contact Carmen Perlas through the parish office at (650) 366-9544 for current meeting times.

Middle School Youth Ministry

For students in grades 5 through 8. Middle School Youth Ministry is a place to explore how God has revealed himself in the world and to become more aware of God’s presence in daily life. Over the year, students learn about God, revelation and faith, Jesus Christ, the Holy Spirit and the Church, the liturgy and sacraments, Christian morality and justice, and prayer. They also explore the lives of the saints, and every topic is connected to their own experience.

Gatherings combine prayer, structured learning, games, and service projects.

High School Youth Ministry

High School Youth Ministry gives high school students a place to explore their faith and become more aware of God’s presence in their lives, with an emphasis on meaningful connections with one another, the parish, and the wider community. The group joins the Peninsula Multifaith Coalition for service projects and for learning from others.

High school students can also prepare for Confirmation.

Next step: Call the parish office to register for youth ministry.

Adults

Keep learning, at your own pace.

Contact Deacon Rich Foley, Adult Faith Formation Coordinator, through the parish office at (650) 366-9544.

Our faith is formed every day by the ways we experience God in our lives. Adult faith formation connects parishioners with learning and prayer opportunities in the parish and with classes, workshops, retreats, and prayer services in the wider Catholic community.

Bible study

Deacon Rich Foley leads Bible study in seasonal series that explore a book or theme of Scripture over several weeks. Sessions meet on Monday mornings at 10:00 a.m. and repeat on Monday evenings at 7:00 p.m. in Fr. Lacey Hall, so you can come to whichever fits your week. No prior Bible study experience is needed; just bring a Bible and a pen.

Exploring the faith for the first time

If you are not yet Catholic, or are Catholic but have not received Confirmation or First Communion, see Becoming Catholic.

Next step: Call the parish office to ask about the next Bible study series.

Becoming Catholic

Thinking about becoming Catholic? You are welcome here.

Contact Deacon Rich Foley or Deacon David Rolandelli through the parish office at (650) 366-9544.

Becoming Catholic is the parish’s process for adults who want to join the Catholic Church. Its formal name is the Order of Christian Initiation of Adults (OCIA), which many people still know as the RCIA (Rite of Christian Initiation of Adults).

Who it is for

  • Catechumens: people who have never been baptized.
  • Candidates: people who were validly baptized in another Christian church.
  • Catholics who were baptized but have not yet received Confirmation or First Communion.

What to expect

Becoming Catholic is a journey of learning, prayer, and conversion, not just a class. Participants meet regularly to learn the fundamental beliefs of the faith and the meaning of the sacraments, especially the Eucharist and the Mass, with time for prayer, discernment, and spiritual formation. The whole parish supports them along the way, and parishioners are welcome to serve on the team that accompanies them.

The process unfolds in four stages:

  • Inquiry: a time to ask questions and learn what the Church believes.
  • Catechumenate: after deciding to join the Church and celebrating the Rite of Acceptance, a time of deeper study and Christian living.
  • Purification and Enlightenment: a focused time of preparation during Lent.
  • Mystagogy: after receiving the sacraments of initiation at the Easter Vigil, a time to go deeper through the Easter season until Pentecost.

Children and teens

Children age 7 and older who have not been baptized prepare for Baptism, Confirmation, and First Communion through a process adapted for children, with their parents taking an active part. See Baptism for older children.

Next step: Call (650) 366-9544 to talk with a deacon. There is no obligation, and every question is welcome.
